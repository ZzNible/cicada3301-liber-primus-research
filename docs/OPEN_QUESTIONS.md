# Open Questions and Useful Community Contributions

The project is currently evidence-gated. These are the kinds of new evidence that could materially change the research state.

## 1. What mechanism produces the unusually low immediate-repeat count?

**SIGNAL:** canonical unresolved LP2 has 86 immediate self-repeats across 12,954 valid adjacent transitions.

**OPEN:** mechanism.

Useful evidence would be an authenticated or tightly provenance-pinned Cicada source that independently fixes a reversible stateful, transition-constrained, repeat-management or related operation **before** it is tested on LP2.

Community solver implementations alone do not satisfy this criterion.

## 2. Does an exact historical AN END target page survive?

Useful contributions include:

- raw Tor crawl exports from 2014–2015;
- historical hidden-service page archives preserving exact bytes;
- operator-held crawl databases;
- old personal solver mirrors with acquisition dates;
- screenshots paired with raw HTML/files;
- exact archive-member provenance;
- correspondence identifying the original locator or page representation.

Please preserve original bytes and metadata. Do not normalize or re-save before hashing.

## 3. Do private 2014 participant artifacts survive?

Several historical routes ended in individualized/private recipient state that is missing from public corpora.

If you participated in the 2012–2014 puzzles and still possess original files, emails, PGP messages, onion downloads, scripts, browser caches, disk images or notes, those objects may be more useful than another ciphertext attack.

Preserve originals and record acquisition context. Do not overwrite timestamps if avoidable.

## 4. Can cross-round reuse be expanded prospectively?

At least one deliberate semantic/representation bridge across campaigns is established.

A useful next result would be another bridge discovered using a rule fixed from source structure rather than by searching many numbers, phrases and files after the fact.

## 5. Pliny / Plinius analogies

No historical Cicada connection to Pliny the Elder or Pliny the Liberator is established.

Two ideas remain suitable for controlled exploration:

- **HYPOTHESIS:** test whether solved Liber Primus sections disproportionately behave as routing/index/meta-instructions, inspired by the structural role of Book I in Pliny the Elder's *Natural History*. This should be evaluated against other comparable philosophical/instructional works, not treated as a name match.
- **OPEN methodological analogy:** use decomposition, representation changes and adversarial boundary probing as search tactics, but require out-of-sample predictions and controls before promoting any result.

## How to contribute

A useful issue should include:

1. what the object/claim is;
2. where it came from;
3. earliest known date;
4. whether bytes are original or transformed;
5. hashes where possible;
6. why it changes an existing `OPEN` or `HOLD` rather than merely suggesting another parameter sweep.

The most valuable contribution may be an old forgotten file rather than a new solver.
